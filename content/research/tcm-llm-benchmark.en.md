## Abstract

Strong performance on general question answering does not establish a language model's understanding of traditional Chinese medicine. Questions about diagnosis, prescriptions, and classical texts involve specialized terminology and knowledge that call for evaluation across factual accuracy, reasoning, and safety.

This research develops a benchmark of more than 600 challenging questions to compare frontier models and study how access to retrieved domain knowledge affects answer reliability. It aims to provide a common basis for assessing model capabilities and retrieval-augmented approaches in specialist knowledge applications.

## Background

Classical medical texts have distinctive terminology, with related knowledge expressed across different editions, phrasings, and document structures. Answering questions requires both understanding the request and identifying relevant knowledge in its proper context. Fluency alone is insufficient to establish the reliability of specialist content.

General benchmarks may not adequately cover these requirements. A shared benchmark spanning diagnosis, prescriptions, and classical text comprehension, evaluated through factual, logical, and safety criteria, can help distinguish model capabilities from the contribution of external knowledge and inform subsequent system improvements.
